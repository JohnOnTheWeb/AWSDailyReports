## Analysis

The September 2026 landscape shows agentic AI moving decisively from experimental to production-ready tooling. The emergence of platforms like Kiro that handle end-to-end development workflows—from requirements gathering through architectural design to implementation—signals a fundamental shift in how software is conceived and built. This evolution parallels the broader platform engineering maturation, where 73% of teams now embed AI directly into their developer workflows rather than treating it as a separate capability.

The measurement challenge has become acute, with expanded DORA metrics specifically addressing AI-generated code velocity versus quality trade-offs. The introduction of deployment rework rate as a fifth core metric reflects industry recognition that traditional speed metrics can be misleading when AI agents generate code faster than testing infrastructure can validate it. This measurement gap has become particularly evident as organizations scale AI coding tools across large engineering teams.

Platform engineering adoption continues to face practical hurdles despite widespread enthusiasm. The revelation that IDPs frequently stall after initial deployment due to overly restrictive golden paths suggests the industry is still learning how to balance developer autonomy with standardization. The most successful implementations appear to be those that preserve developer choice while providing clear, optional acceleration paths rather than mandatory workflows.
